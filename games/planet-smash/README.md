# Planet Smash

Smash real planets, the Sun and the biggest star in the universe with lasers, a railgun, spaceships,
asteroids, an alien ray, the Moon and black holes. The planets are real space-telescope and spacecraft
pictures, and the sky is the real Milky Way.

## How to play

1. Tap **▶ Play**.
2. Pick what to smash. The buttons on the left switch between **🪨 Planets** (Mercury, Venus, Earth, the
   Moon, Mars, Terraformed Mars) and **🔥 Gas Giants & Stars** (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, the Sun,
   and the Biggest Star).
3. **Swipe** to spin the planet round, **pick a weapon** on the right, then **tap** the planet where you
   want it to hit. You can change weapon for every shot. Everything fires from where you are: beams come
   from your cannon, and spaceships, the flying saucer, asteroids and the Moon fly out past you.
   **Pinch** to zoom in and out (zoom right out to see the whole Biggest Star).
4. The **❤️ Planet health** bar shows how close it is to blowing up. Gas giants and stars also get more
   fiery and red as they get hurt.
5. **📸** takes a picture. Press and hold the picture to save it, or tap **Save / Share**.

Keyboard (Mac): arrow keys spin, 1–9 pick a weapon, + / − or the scroll wheel zoom, Space fires at the middle, P takes a picture, Esc
goes back to the planets.

## Weapons

| | What it does |
|---|---|
| 🔴 Laser | small hits |
| 🔵 Plasma Laser | three blue bolts |
| 🌈 Rainbow Laser | very strong, and the only thing that can hurt the Biggest Star |
| ⚡ Railgun | blasts a hole right through the planet and out the other side |
| 🚀 Spaceships | a squadron flies in and shoots |
| ☄️ Asteroid | a rock from space |
| 🛸 Alien Ray | a flying saucer with a green beam |
| 🌕 The Moon | smashes small planets (Mercury, Mars, the Moon) in one hit |
| ⚫ Black Hole | swallows anything except the Biggest Star |

Most planets take several hits. Rocky planets break into rocks and red-hot gas; gas giants, ice giants
and stars blow up in a supernova that leaves a nebula behind (the real Crab Nebula, from the James Webb
Space Telescope).
